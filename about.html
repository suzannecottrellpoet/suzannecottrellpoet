About the Author

Suzanne Cottrell

Also known as Suzanne "Suzy Berry" Cottrell

Suzanne writes across poetry, fiction, and nonfiction, often working at the point where close observation of the natural world meets memory, family, and personal history. Her writing has appeared in literary journals and anthologies for a decade, shaped by patience, attention, and a steady commitment to craft.

Her Writing Life

A writer shaped by attention

Suzanne's work moves comfortably between forms. She writes poetry and flash fiction, hint fiction, and creative nonfiction, most often in the form of personal narratives, along with a small number of mostly unpublished personal essays, often returning to the same wellspring: a moment of close observation, held long enough to reveal something larger. A walk through the woods, a flower opening late in the season, the particular texture of bark on a pine, these become starting points for writing that reaches toward memory, family, and the quiet continuity of lived experience.

She has also written in direct response to visual art, collaborating with painters and photographers whose work has prompted poems and stories of their own. This cross-pollination between image and language runs through much of her published work.

Suzanne's first published piece, "Sole Mate," a work of hint fiction, appeared online in July 2016 through Nailpolish Stories, A Tiny and Colorful Literary Magazine. In the years since, her writing has appeared in more than eighty literary journals and anthologies, a body of work that continues to grow with each new piece she sends into the world.

A Photographer's Eye

Observation that becomes language

Suzanne is also a photographer, and the photographs that often sit alongside her writing are hers. The discipline of waiting for the right light, the right angle, the right moment of stillness, informs the way she builds a poem or shapes a line of prose. Looking closely is, for her, a form of writing before any words appear on the page.
